Hi! I'm Harry — an incoming PhD student at the [MIT Operations Research Center](https://orc.mit.edu/), starting Fall 2026 under [Prof. Dimitris Bertsimas](https://dbertsim.mit.edu/). My given name is _Charidimos_, which is what you'll see on my papers; _Harry_ is the day-to-day version.

I recently completed my MEng in Electrical & Computer Engineering at the [National Technical University of Athens](https://www.ece.ntua.gr/en), where I wrote my diploma thesis with [Prof. Giorgos Stamou](https://www.ece.ntua.gr/en/staff/personalPage/stamou-g) on adaptive multi-agent LLM systems for financial trading. I currently work as an AI engineer building agentic document-analysis systems.

My research interests sit at the intersection of **operations research and machine learning**. On the applied side, I'm drawn to combining optimization and ML to tackle high-impact, real-world problems — particularly in **maritime operations**, where scale, uncertainty, and data richness make it a natural meeting point for the two fields. On the theoretical side, I'm fascinated by the deeper interplay between AI and operations research, and by a question I keep returning to: should how research itself is done take a different direction? Recent results — for example, LLMs producing new constructions for long-open Erdős problems — suggest these models could be more than tools for automation: genuine creative collaborators that propose conjectures, find constructions beyond human intuition, and help shape which questions are even worth pursuing.
